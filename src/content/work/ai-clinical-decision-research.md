## The question

Everyone talks about whether African health workers will accept AI. Almost nobody had measured it at a specific hospital, cadre by cadre. So I did, for my BSc nursing research at Gracious Higher Institute of Excellence, supervised by Mr Boris Eko.

The full title is "Knowledge, Attitude and Factors Influencing the Implementation of Artificial Intelligence in Clinical Decision-Making Among Health Professionals at Buea Regional Hospital."

## How it was done

419 questionnaires went out across six professional cadres at Buea Regional Hospital. 387 came back complete and consented, a 92.4% response rate, above the 377 minimum the sample size calculation required.

The instrument was a custom KAP questionnaire built for a low digital literacy context. That was a deliberate choice. Existing tools ask about AI in computer science vocabulary, which measures whether someone recognises jargon rather than whether they understand what the technology does. Mine tested concrete, awareness-level statements instead. Analysis ran in SPSS with significance at p < 0.05.

## What it found

Knowledge was moderate, averaging 61.3%. Staff scored highest on tangible clinical uses like AI reading medical images, and lowest on governance and the limits of AI autonomy. That is not ignorance about the law so much as an accurate read of a real policy vacuum.

Attitudes were cautiously optimistic. 30.7% positive, 66.9% moderate, and only 2.3% negative. Willingness to use AI ran well ahead of trust in its output, which is a reasonable position when you cannot easily check an AI recommendation against a second opinion.

Then the part that reframed the whole thing. When staff rated what would actually influence their use of AI, the top four were reliable electricity, cost, reliable internet, and access to computers. Fear of losing their job ranked dead last out of eighteen factors.

Almost everyone owned a smartphone at 95.6%, yet only 33.6% had reliable internet at work and 28.2% had a departmental computer. Just 12.9% had ever received formal AI training.

## The conclusion

The staff at Buea Regional Hospital are not resistant to AI. They are under-trained and under-equipped, and they know it.

That changes the local problem from persuasion to provision. Attitude turned out not to be a fixed trait either: positive attitude climbed from 1.8% among those with poor knowledge to 57.1% among those with good knowledge. Exposure raises knowledge, knowledge raises attitude. It can be moved.
