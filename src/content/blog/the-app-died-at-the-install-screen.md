## The reframe

Blood is not scarce in Cameroon. It is unfindable.

That distinction is the whole reason any of this exists. A shortage is a tragedy you absorb. A coordination failure is a thing you can build your way out of.

## What we built first

Ayodah. A real platform, not a pitch. Over its run: more than 400 registered donors, 310 donations, three partner hospitals, five or more NGO partners. It won the Silicon Mountain Challenge in 2022.

By any normal measure it worked.

## How it failed

It died at the install screen.

People downloaded it once and never opened it again. Everything behind that screen functioned. The donor records, the hospital relationships, the volunteer network, all of it.

*[What it felt like watching the retention numbers. How long it took to admit what they meant.]*

## The thing that would not stop

The requests kept coming. They just did not come through the app.

*"Do you know an O negative donor?"* Into my personal WhatsApp. At every hour of the night. Over and over.

*[Some of the specific messages, if you are comfortable sharing them. A time of night. A name you can anonymise.]*

We spent about a year arguing with our own users about where they should be asking for help.

## What we did about it

Stopped arguing. Moved to where they already were.

*[Why that took as long as it did. What you had to let go of to make the call.]*

LifeDrop is Ayodah's network and hospitals rebuilt inside WhatsApp. No app, no login, works on low data, English and French. The ward verifies a request before any donor is alerted. It reached the live pitch stage of MTN's YaMo Pitch Season 4 and placed third in its cohort.

The WhatsApp bot is still a working prototype rather than a deployed service. It ships with the Buea pilot.
