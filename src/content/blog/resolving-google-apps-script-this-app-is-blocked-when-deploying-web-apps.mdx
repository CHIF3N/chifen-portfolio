PyCon Cameroon at the Giraffe Hotel in Yaoundé was three days of exactly the kind of energy that makes you want to go home and build something embarrassingly ambitious the same evening.

The Ndolé at the hotel restaurant was the kind of dish that feels like a hug to the heart — I had it twice. The sessions were sharp. And then there was Pedro Leão: very tall, very Brazilian, very insistent that we stop calling him Sir and stop shrinking in technical discussions like we needed permission to be in the room.

Pedro's session on Google Workspace automation was the one that got me. He walked us through using Apps Script and AI to do things your boss currently does manually — daily reports, Sheets dashboards, automated summaries pushed to Gmail — and made it sound so easy that I was already planning my own version before he'd clicked to the next slide.

The idea: a container-bound web app dashboard wired to a Google Sheet, pulling live project metrics, styled in dark mode, deployed as a Web App so anyone on the team could open it in a browser. Forty-eight hours after PyCon, I had something that looked the part. I clicked **Deploy → New deployment**. I copied the URL. I opened it.

And Google handed me a wall of red text.

> **This app is blocked.**  
> *This app tried to access sensitive info in your Google Account. To keep your account safe, Google blocked this access.*

Not the usual *"Google hasn't verified this app"* screen where you can click Advanced and proceed like an adult. This was a full stop. No bypass button. No Advanced link. Just red, and a brick wall.

---

## The Comedy of Errors That Followed

My first thought, I am not ashamed to say, was the internet connection. I am in Buea. MTN drops at least once a day. Camtel is what it is. Orange is technically there. I switched to hotspot. Same screen. I cleared my local DNS cache. Same screen. I tried a different browser. Same screen.

Then I remembered the multi-account issue Pedro had mentioned offhand — something about Chrome getting confused when you're logged into four Google accounts simultaneously and OAuth tokens start scrambling. Into Incognito I went, logged in with just one account.

Same screen.

I fiddled with the deployment settings. *Execute as: Me* versus *Execute as: User accessing the web app.* I tried both. I redeployed three times. I read the same Stack Overflow thread four times, each time hoping the answer would be different.

It wasn't.

---

## Pedro Came Through on Discourse

I eventually posted in the PyCon Cameroon Discourse group, mildly embarrassed, describing the issue. Pedro replied within the hour. The message was short and surgically correct:

> *"Your script is asking for too much. Apps Script reads your code, sees SpreadsheetApp, and requests full Drive-level permissions by default. Google blocks that for unverified apps. Use @OnlyCurrentDoc and lock your manifest scopes to .currentonly. Read the manifest. It's all in there."*

That was the whole thing. Not a network issue. Not a multi-account collision. **Scope greed.**

When Apps Script sees that you're using `SpreadsheetApp`, it requests `https://www.googleapis.com/auth/spreadsheets` — permission to read and write every spreadsheet in your entire Google account. For an unverified app (which is what yours is, unless you've gone through Google's OAuth verification process, which you haven't), that triggers an automated security block. Google doesn't even give you a chance to explain yourself.

The fix is surgical: tell Apps Script exactly which document it's allowed to touch, and declare your scopes manually in the manifest instead of letting the compiler infer them.

## Making It Work: The Steps Nobody Puts First

There are two things most tutorials bury at the bottom that actually need to happen first.

**Show the manifest.** In the Apps Script editor, go to **Project Settings** and tick *"Show 'appsscript.json' manifest file in editor."* The manifest is hidden by default. You cannot edit what you cannot see.

**Create the script from inside the Sheet.** Go to **Extensions > Apps Script** from within your Google Sheet — not from `script.google.com`. A script created that way is container-bound. It inherits the Sheet's trust context. A standalone script is treated like an untrusted third-party client and gets blocked regardless of what's in the manifest.

Once both are in place, deploy under **Execute as: Me**, paste the URL into a fresh Incognito tab, and the wall of red will be gone.

## What Pedro Was Actually Telling Us

There's a broader point in all of this that Pedro made explicitly and I think about often.

When the red screen appeared, my first instinct was to assume I'd done something wrong that I simply wasn't qualified to fix. That the error meant I didn't belong doing this kind of work. That I should close the tab and go back to whatever I was doing before.

That is exactly what he told us not to do.

*Read the manifest. Inspect the scopes. The information is there. You are not missing some secret that is only available to people at companies with T-shirts and ergonomic chairs.*

The scope greed was fixable in two lines of code. The wall was intimidating, not insurmountable. Claiming your seat at the table means staying in your chair when Google shows you a brick wall, reading what the error actually says, and fixing it.

Drop the Sir. Read the manifest. Ship the thing.
